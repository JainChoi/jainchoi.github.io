My research spans international relations, political psychology, public opinion, and computational social science. I study how marginalization and discrimination shape solidarity and foreign-policy attitudes, and how changing power relations—especially China's rise and U.S.–China competition—reshape international behavior and institutions.

Dissertation Project

  • Marginalization and Foreign Policy Attitudes

    How does marginalization at home shape concern for people beyond one's own community and country? This dissertation examines whether and how perceived marginalization and experiences of discrimination influence public attitudes toward foreign policy. It theorizes two competing pathways: experiences of suffering may foster recognition of shared vulnerability and extend solidarity across social and national boundaries, but they may also generate distrust, competition, or exclusion. The project studies these mechanisms across attitudes toward foreign aid, military intervention, economic sanctions, and immigration using cross-national survey data and original survey experiments.

Working Papers

  • Forecasting African Countries' Co-Participation with China in UN Peacekeeping Operations

    Status: Presented at SPSA 2025 and SoWEPS 2025.

    What explains African countries' participation in UN peacekeeping operations alongside China? Using country-year data from 1990 to 2020, this paper examines whether China–Africa economic ties are associated with co-participation in peacekeeping operations. It combines trade-based measures with pooled and fixed-effects logistic regression and random-forest models, framing the analysis as a forecasting and association exercise rather than a causal test.

  • Solidarity or Exclusion? Discrimination Involving Sexuality and Attitudes toward Immigrants in Europe

    Status: Presented at MPSA 2026 and EPSA 2026; scheduled for PRIEC 2026, APSA 2026, and MAPOR 2026.

    Do experiences of discrimination foster solidarity with other marginalized groups, or reinforce exclusion? Using European Social Survey Round 10 data from 31 countries, this paper examines whether discrimination involving sexuality is associated with attitudes toward immigrants. Bayesian hierarchical ordered-logit models estimate evaluations of immigrants' effects on the economy, culture, and their country. The findings are consistent with an interminority solidarity account.
